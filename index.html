Video Title

Connor McDavid uses his speed, goes bar-down

In this short, sub-one-minute highlight, Connor McDavid picks up a loose puck in the neutral zone, uses linear crossovers to build massive speed, attacks the offensive blue line, beats the defenseman by pushing the puck past their stick (attacking the triangle), and fires a wrist shot bar-down without breaking stride.

🏒Identified Skills & Tactics

Individual Skills

  • Forward Skating

    Explosive acceleration and linear crossovers to build speed through the neutral zone.

  • Puck Control

    Stickhandling at top speed while keeping the puck pushed out in front (puck protection).

  • Shooting

    Executing a high-velocity wrist shot in-stride (without gliding or stopping your feet).

Individual Tactics

  • Offensive - Speed Entry

    Attacking the blue line with maximum pace to back off the defender and create a gap.

  • Offensive - Attacking the Triangle

    Pushing the puck through the space between the defender's skates and stick to bypass them.

  • Offensive - Driving the Seam

    Cutting inside the dot-line to improve the shooting angle.

📋Chain of Events: 6-Drill Practice Design

Each drawing is a representative full-ice setup and can be mirrored to run from either end. The routes emphasize the key teaching objective rather than exact player spacing.

Diagram key: Forward skating / skating with puck Defender's backward route Puck movement / shot Puck Cone or triangle aid
1
Skill Isolation

Linear Crossover Acceleration

Setup

Players line up on the goal line. No pucks.

Action

On the whistle, players skate goal line to blue line using only linear crossovers (alternating left over right, right over left) to maximize acceleration.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Maintain a deep knee bend with the chest over the toes.
  2. Drive fully through each crossover edge to create forward power.
  3. Recover the feet quickly and accelerate through the blue line.
2
Skill Isolation

Top-Speed Puck Control

Setup

Cones are placed in a wide, staggered formation down the length of the ice.

Action

Players run the same route, this time with a puck. They must push the puck out in front of them to maintain top speed, rather than stickhandling tightly in their feet.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Push the puck into open space ahead instead of carrying it in the feet.
  2. Keep the head up and use soft hands while skating at top speed.
  3. Skate through every puck touch and avoid gliding around the cones.
3
Skill Isolation

In-Stride Shooting

Setup

Players start at the red line with pucks.

Action

Players accelerate over the blue line and must release a wrist shot from the top of the circles while their feet are still moving.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Keep the feet moving through the release with no glide before the shot.
  2. Transfer weight on the fly while keeping the hands away from the body.
  3. Keep the eyes up and use a quick, compact follow-through to the target.
4
Tactic Integration

Attacking the Triangle

Setup

Place a "stick-handling triangle" aid (or a couple of cones with a stick laid across them) just inside the offensive blue line to simulate a defenseman's reach.

Action

Players enter the zone with speed, push the puck under/around the obstacle, accelerate past it, and shoot in-stride.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Read the defender's feet and stick position before selecting the lane.
  2. Push the puck beyond the stick's reach with one controlled touch.
  3. Accelerate through the move and recover the puck without breaking stride.
5
Tactic Integration

1-on-1 Gap Control (Offensive Focus)

Setup

A forward starts in the neutral zone with a puck; a defenseman starts at the center ice red line, skating backward.

Action

The forward tries to replicate the McDavid entry—using speed to back off the defender, attacking their feet/stick, and trying to cut inside for a shot.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Enter with pace and change speed to force the defender to adjust the gap.
  2. Read the defender's hips and stick, then attack the available triangle.
  3. Protect the puck, drive inside the dot-line, and finish with the feet moving.
6
Game-Like Scenario

Transition Breakout to 1-on-1

Setup

Coach dumps a puck into the neutral zone. Forward and Defenseman start on the whistle from their own end.

Action

The forward must race to pick up the loose puck in the neutral zone (simulating the game clip), immediately turn up-ice, and attack the defenseman 1-on-1 going the other way.

3 Key Teaching Points
  1. Shoulder-check before reaching the loose puck to locate pressure and space.
  2. Make the first touch up-ice and accelerate immediately after retrieval.
  3. Attack the defender before the gap can close and finish the play at speed.
